Digital Sovereignty: Why Your Attention Is the Most Valuable Asset You Will Ever Own

By Vishal Hingol June 1, 2026 6 min read

The Economy You Didn't Agree to Join

At some point in the last decade, attention became currency. Every platform that asks nothing for entry — every free app, every social media feed, every news notification — is charging you something. It is charging you your focus.

The business model is straightforward: keep you engaged as long as possible, sell that engagement to advertisers. The product is not the content. The product is you — specifically, your attention, packaged and sold by the millisecond.

This is not a conspiracy theory. It is the published business model of every major technology platform currently operating.

What It Actually Costs

The average person now spends between 6 and 7 hours per day on screens — not counting work. Research on attention fragmentation shows that after a single notification interruption, the average person takes 23 minutes to return to deep focus. Most people receive dozens of notifications per hour.

The arithmetic is simple. If you can never sustain more than a few minutes of uninterrupted focus, you cannot do deep work. If you cannot do deep work, you cannot produce anything that requires sustained thought. If you cannot produce anything that requires sustained thought, you are limited to consuming what others have produced — which is exactly what the platforms want.

The Algorithm Is Not Neutral

Recommendation algorithms are optimised for engagement, not for your benefit. Outrage, fear, comparison and dopamine-triggering content drive engagement. Calm, nuanced, constructive content does not. The result is a feed tuned to activate your threat response — because threatened people stay on the platform.

This is not accidental. The engineering is deliberate. And it works.

What Digital Sovereignty Actually Means

Digital sovereignty is not about deleting your accounts or living offline. It is about deciding — consciously, deliberately — which technologies serve you and which ones are consuming you.

It means setting the terms of your own engagement rather than accepting the terms the platform has set for you. It means protecting periods of uninterrupted focus. It means choosing what enters your attention rather than allowing others to choose for you.

Practically: notification-free hours. Chosen consumption windows rather than open feeds. Deliberate content selection rather than algorithmic curation. The ability to be bored without immediately reaching for a screen.

This is harder than it sounds. These systems are engineered by the world's best behavioural scientists and funded by trillion-dollar companies. But it is possible. And the return — in clarity, productivity, and quality of thought — is unlike anything else available.

Your attention is finite. It is also the material from which you build everything that matters in your life. Treat it accordingly.
